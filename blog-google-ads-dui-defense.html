Google Ads for DUI Defense Attorneys: Win the Urgent Searches That Become Retained Clients

VL
Vertex Law MarketingPublished 20 November 2026 · 10 min read

A person arrested for DUI does not research lawyers the way most clients research anything. They search scared, on a phone, often from a police station parking lot or a kitchen table in the middle of the night. They type “DUI lawyer near me” and they call the first firm that looks able to help — usually within minutes, and usually without visiting more than one or two websites.

That urgency is exactly why Google Ads works so well for DUI defense — and exactly why it can burn money faster than almost any other kind of law firm advertising. DUI keywords are expensive, the searcher is in crisis, and every click from the wrong person is a click a real client never got. The firms that win are not the ones with the biggest budget. They are the ones whose campaigns are built for the way a person facing a DUI charge actually searches, clicks, and calls.

Here is how DUI defense firms use Google Ads to turn arrest searches into retained clients — and how to stop paying for clicks that were never going to become clients.

1. Accept that the search happens in a crisis window — then build for it

A DUI arrest creates one of the shortest decision windows in all of legal marketing. The searcher has just been arrested, or someone they love has. They may have a court date already, or a license suspension letter on the way. They are not comparing five firms over a week. They are choosing one firm tonight, and the deciding factors are speed, clarity, and whether a real person answers the phone.

That means the campaign has to be judged on a different clock than most Google Ads work. A slow landing page, a form that asks for a case number before it offers help, or a phone number buried below the fold does not just lower the conversion rate — it loses the client to the firm whose number was one tap away. Our guide to DUI defense marketing covers the full picture of how these clients decide; for Google Ads, the practical consequence is simple.

Every part of the campaign — the keyword, the ad, the landing page, and the phone answering — has to work for a frightened person on a phone at 2 a.m. If any one of those four fails, the click you paid for becomes someone else’s client.

2. Bid on searches that can become clients — in the right order

Not every DUI search is worth the same money. The highest-intent searches name the charge and the lawyer in the same breath: “DUI lawyer Chicago”, “DUI attorney near me”, “drunk driving lawyer”, “first offense DUI lawyer”. Those are the searches to win first, and to bid most aggressively on, because the searcher has already decided they need a lawyer and is choosing which one to call.

Below those, a second tier of searches is still valuable but should be bid on separately, at lower bids, with different ads: “what happens after a DUI arrest”, “first DUI penalties”, “can you refuse a breathalyzer”, “how much does a DUI lawyer cost”. These searchers are earlier in their decision. They may become clients — often after a consultation that answers the question honestly — but they convert at a lower rate, and they should not share a budget with the searches that are ready to call now.

Keep the two tiers in separate campaigns with separate budgets. If they share one budget, the cheaper informational clicks will quietly consume the money that should have bought the urgent ones. And remember that paid search does not work alone: our guide to ranking in the Google Map Pack covers the local visibility system, and the firms that win both channels own the first screen for DUI searches in their city.

  • Highest intent: DUI or DWI or OUI + lawyer or attorney + city, and “near me” versions of the same. Bid here first.
  • High intent: specific situations — “first offense DUI”, “second DUI”, “DUI with accident”, “underage DUI”, “CDL DUI”, “DUI license suspension”. Separate ad groups, matched landing pages.
  • Research intent: penalties, costs, license consequences, “do I need a lawyer for a first DUI”. Lower bids, helpful landing pages, and a clear path to a free consultation.
  • Not worth bidding on: free legal advice, legal aid, pro bono, DUI classes and ignition interlock devices, jobs, and do-it-yourself court forms. These belong on your negative keyword list, not in your campaigns.

3. Match the ad to the charge, not to the firm

Most DUI ads read the same: the firm name, “free consultation”, “aggressive defense”, and a phone number. A searcher who has just been arrested skims past that, because it does not tell them whether this firm handles their situation — a first offense, a second offense, a commercial license at risk, a refusal charge.

Build one ad group per situation — first offense DUI, repeat DUI, DUI with accident or injury, underage DUI, CDL and commercial drivers, license suspension and refusal cases — and write the ads for that situation alone. A first-offense ad should speak to a first offense: what happens next, what the license consequences are, and that the consultation is free and confidential. A CDL ad should say commercial license, because a driver whose livelihood depends on that license is looking for a firm that understands what is at stake.

  • Use the searcher’s words in the headline. If the search was “DUI lawyer”, the headline should say DUI lawyer, not “arrested? We can help”.
  • Say what happens when they call. A free, confidential consultation, what to bring to it, and a real person answering — not a form and a wait until morning.
  • Use call assets and location assets. On mobile, most DUI clicks should become phone calls, not website visits. Make the phone number one tap away, and show the office location so the searcher knows the firm is local and can reach the courthouse.
  • Test call-only ads for the highest-intent searches. When someone searches “DUI lawyer near me” at 1 a.m. from a phone, the fastest path to a retained client is a call, not a landing page. Call-only ads remove the step in between.
  • Keep the tone steady and honest. Fear-based copy and promises about outcomes repel the careful clients you want most — and in many states, advertising rules limit what a firm can promise about results. Say what you will do: listen, review the facts, explain the options honestly, and defend the case as strongly as the law allows.

The same rule applies to the page the ad leads to. An ad about first-offense DUI that lands on a general criminal defense homepage is a broken promise, and a searcher in crisis will not wait to work out where the DUI information lives.

4. Build landing pages that convert a scared visitor in seconds

A DUI click is an expensive click, and the landing page has to earn it immediately, because the visitor is frightened, distracted, and comparing you against two other firms in other tabs.

The first screen should answer three things before anything else: do you handle my kind of DUI case, what does it cost to talk to you, and how fast will someone respond. A phone number that is one tap away, a short form — name, phone, and one line about what happened — and a plain statement that the consultation is free and confidential. Everything else — attorney biographies, office photos, explanations of DUI law — belongs further down, after the visitor has decided the firm might be able to help.

  • One page per situation. The first-offense page talks about first offenses: what usually happens after the arrest, the license timeline, what the first consultation covers. The repeat-offense page does the same for repeat cases. A page that genuinely answers the searcher’s question is the page that converts — and it is also the page Google rewards with a better quality score, which lowers what you pay per click.
  • Load fast on a phone. A slow page on a mobile connection loses the click you just paid for. Keep images light, remove anything that delays the phone number and the form, and test the page on a real phone, not only on a desktop.
  • Show real people. Real photos of the attorneys and staff, a real office address, and a real phone number. Stock photos of gavels tell a frightened visitor nothing — and in this practice area, anonymity reads as a warning sign.
  • Do not ask for what you do not need yet. Arrest reports, breath test results, and court paperwork come after the first conversation, not before it. A form that asks for them up front signals a firm that does not understand the visitor’s situation.
  • Confirm what happens next. After the form is submitted, say exactly what happens: who will call, when, and what they will ask. A confirmation page that says nothing is a missed chance to reassure someone who is already anxious.

5. Cut the wasted spend with negative keywords

In DUI defense, the difference between a profitable campaign and a money pit is often the negative keyword list. Because the core terms are expensive, a single irrelevant click costs real money — and the irrelevant searches are numerous, from people looking for DUI classes to job seekers and students.

Start with the obvious exclusions and review your search terms report every week to find more:

  • Jobs and education: jobs, hiring, salary, law school, paralegal, internship, “how to become a DUI lawyer”
  • Classes and devices: DUI classes, alcohol education programs, ignition interlock device cost and installation, SR-22 insurance quotes — unless your firm genuinely offers those services
  • Free and DIY: free legal advice, pro bono, legal aid, “beat a DUI without a lawyer”, court forms, how to represent yourself in a DUI case
  • Research and news: DUI statistics, famous DUI arrests, celebrity cases, news stories, “what is a DUI”
  • Wrong places: cities and states you do not serve, and the names of firms you are not — unless you are deliberately and carefully running a competitor campaign, which is a separate decision with its own rules
  • Wrong charges: if you do not handle drug possession or domestic violence cases, exclude those terms rather than paying for their clicks when they ride along with a DUI search

The search terms report is where this work happens. Every week, look at what people actually typed before they clicked your ad, and add every term that could not have become a client to the negative list. Over a month, this single habit can cut a meaningful share of wasted spend — and the savings go straight into the searches that do become clients.

6. Win the call: 24/7 intake is the real conversion rate

DUI clients call the firm that answers. A searcher who has just been arrested will often call two or three firms in a row; the one that picks up first usually gets the case. That makes intake speed — not the ad copy, not the bid — the single biggest lever on your cost per retained client.

  • Answer in minutes, around the clock. DUI arrests happen at night, on weekends, and on holidays, and so do the searches. A real response standard — every call answered live during the day, and a trained answering service or on-call intake after hours — is worth more than a higher bid.
  • Keep the first call short and human. The caller needs to hear that the firm handles DUI cases, that the consultation is free and confidential, and what the next step is. A script that asks for documents before it offers reassurance loses callers.
  • Follow up once, well. A caller who does not answer the first callback is not a lost client. One prompt, polite follow-up — a call and a short message — recovers cases a single attempt would lose. More than that becomes pressure.
  • Track the call, not just the click. Call tracking numbers per campaign, and a note in your intake system of which campaign and keyword produced each call, turn Google Ads from a traffic bill into a client pipeline you can judge.

This is also where many firms discover their ads were working all along and their intake was not. Before raising the budget, check how many calls went unanswered, how long callbacks took, and how many consultations never got booked. Fixing intake is usually cheaper than buying more clicks.

7. Target the geography and the hours where DUI arrests happen

DUI defense is a local practice, and your campaigns should be built that way. A firm in one city should not be paying for clicks in another state, and a firm that serves three counties should know which county produces its cases.

  • Target where you actually practice. Set location targeting to the cities and counties you serve, and use “people in your targeted locations” rather than “people searching about your locations”, so a searcher in another state looking up your city does not cost you a click.
  • Bid by location performance. When the data shows one county retains clients at a lower cost than another, shift budget toward it. Over a quarter, that reallocation alone can move your cost per retained client noticeably.
  • Schedule ads for when people search — and when you can answer. DUI searches spike late at night and after weekend arrests, but if no one can answer the phone at 3 a.m., paying for those clicks wastes them. Either staff the hours or schedule the ads around the hours you can genuinely serve, and use call scheduling so the phone number only shows when someone is there to pick up.
  • Separate mobile from desktop. Most DUI searches happen on a phone, and mobile clicks convert differently — usually into calls, at a different rate and cost. Keep mobile and desktop performance visible separately so you are not judging a call-heavy mobile campaign by desktop form-fill standards.

8. Budget by retained client, not by click

The question most DUI firms ask first — how much should we spend on Google Ads — is the wrong question until you know what a retained client is worth to the firm and what you are willing to pay to sign one. Our guide to how much a law firm should spend on Google Ads gives the budgeting framework in full; for DUI defense, three rules matter most.

  • Work back from the client, not forward from the click. Decide what a retained DUI client is worth and what you can afford to pay to sign one, then judge the campaign by cost per retained client. A campaign with expensive clicks and a low cost per retained client is a good campaign; a campaign with cheap clicks and no retained clients is a waste of money.
  • Give a new campaign enough budget to learn. A budget so small that it buys a handful of clicks a day will never produce enough data to judge, and pausing it after a week tells you nothing. Fund it properly for a full month, with the structure in place, before deciding whether it works.
  • Reallocate, do not just increase. When one situation signs clients at a lower cost than another, move budget toward it. When one keyword produces calls that never retain, pause it and give the money to the keywords that do. Most DUI campaigns improve more from reallocation than from a bigger total budget.

Judge the spend monthly, not daily. Click costs move with competition, and a single expensive night means nothing. A month of cost per consultation and cost per retained client, by campaign and by situation, tells you what to expand, what to fix, and what to stop.

9. Measure retained clients, not clicks

Google Ads reports clicks, impressions, and click-through rates by default. None of those is a client. For a DUI defense firm, the numbers that matter are consultations booked, consultations that become retained clients, and cost per retained client — by campaign, by situation, and by keyword where the data allows.

  • Connect the ad to the retained client. Use call tracking, form tracking, and your intake or case management system together, so a retained client can be traced back to the campaign that produced it. Without that link, you are guessing which half of the budget works.
  • Import offline conversions. A click becomes a consultation, and a consultation becomes a retained client, days later. Feeding retained clients back into Google Ads as offline conversions lets the system bid toward the searches that actually produce clients, not just the ones that produce clicks.
  • Review search terms weekly, performance monthly. The search terms report catches wasted spend early; the monthly review of cost per retained client is where the real decisions happen — which situations to grow, which keywords to pause, and where the next dollar goes.
  • Keep the rest of the funnel honest. Ads bring the call, but the website, the intake, and the follow-up retain the client. Where to start this month

You do not need to rebuild everything at once. For most small-to-midsize DUI defense firms, this order returns the most retained clients for the least spend:

  • This week: split your highest-intent keywords into their own campaign with matched ads and landing pages, add the obvious negative keywords, and check how many calls went unanswered last week
  • This month: rebuild your top three landing pages — first offense, repeat offense, and license suspension — with one-tap calling, a short form, and a clear statement of what happens next; start reviewing the search terms report weekly
  • This quarter: connect retained clients back to campaigns with call tracking and offline conversion imports, then reallocate budget toward the situations with the lowest cost per retained client

DUI clients are not looking for the loudest firm in their city. They are looking for the firm that answers, understands their situation, explains what it costs — a free, confidential consultation — and follows up when it matters. Be that firm in the ad, on the page, and on the phone, and Google Ads stops being the most expensive line in your marketing budget and starts being the most reliable source of retained clients you have.

Key Takeaways

  • DUI searches happen in a crisis window: the firm that answers first, with a clear next step, usually retains the client.
  • Split intent into tiers: bid hardest on DUI + lawyer + city searches, lower on research questions, and never let the two share one budget.
  • One ad group per situation — first offense, repeat offense, CDL, license suspension — with ads and landing pages written for that situation alone.
  • Negative keywords and a weekly search terms review are the fastest way to cut wasted spend in DUI campaigns.
  • 24/7 intake is the real conversion rate: staff the hours your ads run, or schedule the ads around the hours you can answer.
  • Target the places you practice, schedule ads around the hours you can answer, and judge mobile and desktop performance separately.
  • Budget by cost per retained client, not cost per click — and feed retained clients back into Google Ads as offline conversions so the system bids toward real clients.
