Local SEO for Law Firms: How to Rank in the Google Map Pack

VL
Vertex Law MarketingPublished May 14, 2026 · 11 min read

When someone searches "personal injury lawyer near me," the first thing they see isn't a website — it's the map with three firms listed beneath it. That block is called the Map Pack, and for local law firms it's the single most valuable piece of real estate on Google. This is how it works and how you win a spot in it.

Local SEO is different from the SEO most people picture. It isn't only about ranking a webpage — it's about ranking your business in Google's local index so you appear on the map, in "near me" searches, and on Google Maps itself. For a law firm whose clients are almost always local, this is where the highest-intent traffic lives. Someone searching "DUI attorney [your city]" isn't researching a term paper. They need a lawyer, and they need one now.

Why the Map Pack matters more than anything else

The Map Pack — the three local business listings Google shows with a map — sits above the traditional blue-link results for almost every "[practice area] + [city]" or "near me" search. Because it appears first and carries visual trust signals like star ratings and review counts, it captures a large share of the clicks before a searcher ever scrolls to organic results.

For a law firm, the practical implication is simple: you can have a beautiful website ranking #1 in the organic results and still lose the majority of local clients to the three firms sitting above you in the Map Pack. Winning local search means winning that block first.

3 listings

That's all the Map Pack shows. Everyone else is one tap away on "more places" — which is why the difference between position 3 and position 4 in local search can be the difference between a full calendar and a quiet phone.

The three factors Google uses to rank local firms

Google has been consistent that local rankings come down to three broad factors. Understanding them tells you exactly where to put your effort.

Relevance
How well your listing matches what someone searched — practice area, services, and category
Distance
How close your office is to the searcher or the location in their query
Prominence
How well-known and trusted your firm is — reviews, links, citations, and reputation

You can't move your office closer to every searcher, so distance is largely fixed. That means your real leverage is in relevance and prominence — and both are things you can actively improve. The rest of this guide is about doing exactly that.

Step 1: Master your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (the listing that powers the Map Pack) is the foundation of everything. A fully optimized profile beats a half-finished one almost every time. Make sure you:

  • Choose the most specific primary category. "Personal injury attorney" is far stronger for that practice than the generic "Lawyer." Add secondary categories for your other practice areas.
  • Get your name, address, and phone (NAP) exactly right — and identical everywhere it appears online. Inconsistent NAP data is one of the most common quiet killers of local rankings.
  • Fill in every field: hours, service areas, attorney photos, office photos, services, and a keyword-aware business description.
  • Post regularly. Google Business Profile posts signal an active, legitimate business — case results, FAQs, and updates all help.

A firm that treats its profile as a living asset consistently outranks one that set it up years ago and forgot about it.

Step 2: Build a review engine, not a review request

Reviews are one of the strongest prominence signals in local search, and they're the first thing a potential client actually reads. Both the quantity and recency of reviews matter — a firm with a steady stream of recent five-star reviews signals trust in a way a firm with a handful of old ones cannot.

The firms that win here don't ask for reviews occasionally; they build a repeatable system:

  • Ask every satisfied client, at the natural high point of their matter, in person and by follow-up.
  • Make it frictionless — send a direct link to your review page by text or email.
  • Respond to every review, positive or negative. Thoughtful responses signal an engaged firm and reassure future clients reading along.

Step 3: Create location and practice-area pages

Relevance is won on your website too. Google needs to clearly understand what you do and where you do it. A single "Practice Areas" page that lists everything in a paragraph doesn't cut it. Instead:

  • Give every major practice area its own dedicated page with genuinely useful content — what the process looks like, what a client should expect, and answers to the questions they actually ask.
  • Create city or location pages if you serve multiple areas, each written for that specific community rather than copy-pasted with the city name swapped.
  • Answer real questions. Content that addresses "how long does a personal injury case take" or "what happens at a DUI arraignment" earns both rankings and trust, and increasingly gets surfaced by AI search too.

Step 4: Earn citations and local links

Prominence is also built off your site. Two forms of external validation move local rankings:

  • Citations — consistent listings of your firm's NAP on legal directories (Avvo, Justia, FindLaw), local business directories, and your local bar association. Consistency is everything here.
  • Local links — links from local news, community organizations, sponsorships, and legitimate legal resources. A handful of quality local links outweighs a pile of low-value ones.

You don't need thousands of links. For most local law firms, a steady drip of relevant, high-quality citations and links compounds into durable rankings over time.

Common local SEO mistakes that quietly tank rankings

Sometimes the fastest way to rank higher is to stop doing the things that hold you back. These are the errors we see most often when auditing law firm local presence:

  • Inconsistent NAP data. Your firm listed as "Suite 200" in one place and "Ste. 200" in another, an old phone number lingering on a directory, a former address never updated. Google reads these inconsistencies as uncertainty about who and where you are, and it costs you rankings.
  • The wrong primary category. Choosing "Lawyer" instead of your specific practice category leaves ranking power on the table for the searches that matter most.
  • Keyword-stuffing your business name. Adding "Best Personal Injury Lawyers" to your Google Business Profile name violates Google's guidelines and can get your listing suspended. Use your real business name.
  • Ignoring reviews — or worse, buying fake ones. A stagnant review profile stalls your rankings; fake reviews risk removal and reputational damage. There's no shortcut here.
  • A slow, non-mobile-friendly website. Most local legal searches happen on phones. A site that's slow or hard to use on mobile undermines both rankings and conversions.

Fixing these often produces visible movement faster than any amount of new link-building, because you're removing the friction that was suppressing the authority you already have.

Track your rankings so you know it's working

Local SEO is measurable, and you should hold it to that standard. Because Map Pack results vary by the searcher's exact location, tracking is a little different from traditional SEO:

  • Watch your rankings by location, not just a single position. A tool that maps where you rank across your service area shows you exactly where you're strong and where you're invisible.
  • Monitor Google Business Profile insights — how many people found you, called, requested directions, or visited your site. These are direct signals of real-world results.
  • Tie it back to cases. The point isn't rankings for their own sake; it's signed clients. Track how many new matters originate from local search so you can see the return.

How long does local SEO take?

Honestly? Longer than paid ads, and that's the trade-off. Where Google Ads can produce leads the day it launches, local SEO typically takes three to six months to show meaningful movement and continues compounding well beyond that. The upside is that the traffic is essentially free once you rank, and it doesn't disappear the moment you pause spend. The smartest firms run both: paid search for immediate cases while local SEO builds the durable foundation underneath.

Key Takeaways

  • The Map Pack shows only three firms and sits above organic results — it's the most valuable local real estate on Google.
  • Local rankings come down to relevance, distance, and prominence; your leverage is in relevance and prominence.
  • A fully optimized Google Business Profile with the right categories and consistent NAP is the foundation.
  • Reviews — in volume and recency — are among the strongest signals and the first thing clients read.
  • Dedicated practice-area and location pages win relevance; citations and local links build prominence.
  • Expect three to six months for meaningful results — but the traffic compounds and doesn't vanish when you stop paying.
