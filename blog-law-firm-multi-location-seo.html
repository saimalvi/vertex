Law Firm SEO for Multiple Locations: How to Rank in Every City You Serve

VL
Vertex Law MarketingPublished 28 November 2026 · 10 min read

A firm with three offices calls us with the same complaint every time: “We rank number one in our home city, but we are invisible everywhere else.” Their website is fine. Their reviews are good. Their Google Business Profile is verified. But in the two other cities they serve, they do not appear in the Map Pack, they do not appear on page one, and the phones in those offices stay quiet.

The reason is simple, and most multi-location firms miss it: Google does not rank firms. It ranks locations. Every city you serve is a separate local search market, with its own Map Pack, its own competitors, and its own evidence of who actually practices there. A firm that treats its second and third cities as an afterthought — a line on the contact page, a swapped city name on a copied page — gets treated as an afterthought by Google too.

Here is how multi-location law firms win local searches in every city they serve.

1. One Google Business Profile per real office — and only per real office

Every genuine office location gets its own Google Business Profile, with its own address, phone number, hours, photos, and reviews. That is the foundation of multi-location local SEO, and it is also where most firms create their biggest problems.

A profile needs a real, staffed office where clients can meet you. A virtual office, a shared coworking desk you visit once a month, a UPS mailbox, or a staff member’s home address will not hold up: Google suspends profiles that cannot be verified at a real location, and competitors report them. If you have two offices, you get two profiles. If you serve five cities from one office, you get one profile — and the other four cities are won through your website, not through extra profiles.

Each profile should be complete and distinct: the correct office address and local phone number, photos of that actual office and the attorneys who work there, hours that match that office, and the practice areas that office handles. A profile with stock photos and a copied description signals to Google — and to clients — that the location is not quite real.

2. Give every city its own location page — with genuine local detail

For each city you serve, build one strong location page. Not a paragraph on the contact page, and not a copy of your home-city page with the city name swapped out. A real location page answers the question a person in that city is actually asking: does this firm practice here, and do they know this place?

A strong location page includes the office address and a map, the attorneys who work from that office, the practice areas handled there, and genuine local detail: the courthouse where cases are heard, the local rules or procedures that matter, and the neighborhoods and surrounding towns the office serves. A personal injury page for your second city should mention the highways and intersections where your clients’ accidents happen. A criminal defense page should name the county courthouse and the local procedures a person arrested there will face.

What does not work is publishing near-identical pages for twenty towns with only the town name changed. Thin, duplicated location pages do not rank, and Google treats them as spam. One honest, detailed page per place you genuinely serve beats a wall of copy-and-paste. 3. Build the practice-area matrix honestly — or do not build it at all

A firm with three offices and four practice areas can quickly talk itself into twelve location-by-practice-area pages, then thirty, then sixty. Most of those pages end up thin, because the firm has nothing different to say about family law in city B than it said about family law in city A.

Build the matrix only where you have something real to say. If your second office handles personal injury and criminal defense, build strong pages for those two — and do not build a family law page for that office just to complete the set. Each page needs its own substance: the local court, the local procedure, the attorneys who handle those cases there, and the questions clients in that city actually ask.

A useful rule: if two pages would say the same thing with only the city and practice area swapped, publish one of them. Search engines reward depth in a few pages far more than thinness across many, and a prospective client can tell the difference in seconds.

4. Earn reviews at each office, not just at headquarters

Reviews are a local ranking factor, and Google counts them per profile, not per firm. A firm with forty reviews on its main office profile and two on its second office has, in Google’s eyes, a well-reviewed firm in one city and an unproven one in the other.

Ask for reviews at the office where the work was done, and make it easy: a direct link to that office’s review page, sent soon after the matter closes, while the client still remembers the attorney’s name. Do not route every client to the main profile. And respond to reviews on every profile, in the voice of that office — a reply that mentions the city and the attorney involved reads as genuine, and a copied reply reads as a template.

Never buy reviews, never trade favors for them, and never write them yourself. Fabricated reviews breach advertising rules in many states, and they are the first thing a competitor reports. If a newer office has few reviews, that is fine: honest reviews accumulate, and a small number of genuine ones beats a large number of suspicious ones.

5. Fix the citation mess multi-location firms inherit

Every office accumulates listings over the years: directories, bar association pages, old addresses from before a move, a phone number from two rebrands ago, a listing for an office that closed in 2019. For a single-office firm that is a nuisance. For a multi-location firm it is a ranking problem, because Google cross-checks your name, address, and phone number across the web — and conflicting information makes it less confident about where you actually are.

Audit every location: search the firm name plus each city, and check the directories, legal listings, and data aggregators that come up. Correct the address and phone number for every live office, and remove or correct listings for offices that no longer exist. Keep one consistent format for the firm name and each office’s address everywhere it appears.

6. Win local links in every city, not just your home town

Local links tell Google a firm belongs in a city. A personal injury firm that sponsors a youth league in its home town, belongs to that city’s bar association, and gets quoted in that city’s paper has local authority there — and none of it transfers to the second city, where the firm is a stranger.

Each office needs its own local footprint: the local bar association and its sections, the chamber of commerce, local sponsorships and community involvement, and relationships with local press and organizations. None of this has to be elaborate. A bar association membership, a chamber listing, a sponsorship of a local event, and a genuinely useful comment to a local reporter all count — and they accumulate into a local profile Google can see.

The same principle applies to content. A blog post about a change in local court procedure, a new local ordinance, or a high-profile local case — written with real local knowledge — earns links and attention in that city in a way a generic post never will.

7. Add local schema markup to every location page

Schema markup is the structured data that helps search engines understand what a page is about. For a multi-location firm, it is how you tell Google, in a format it cannot misunderstand, that each office is a real place with a real address, a real phone number, and real hours.

Each location page should carry Attorney or LegalService schema with that office’s name, address, phone number, hours, and the areas it serves. Each office’s Google Business Profile and its location page should agree on every detail — the same name format, the same address format, the same phone number. Inconsistency between the two is one of the most common reasons a second location underperforms.

This is technical work, and it is easy to get wrong: one schema block copied across every location page with the wrong address in it does more harm than no schema at all. 8. Answer the phone like a local firm in every city

Ranking in a second city is only half the job. A caller from that city who reaches a receptionist who has never heard of their town, cannot name the local courthouse, and transfers them three times will not become a client — and will leave a review that says so.

Each office needs an intake process that knows its own city: the courthouse, the local procedure, the attorneys in that office, and the next step for a caller from that area. A local phone number on each profile and each location page helps — clients trust a number with their area code, and it signals to Google that the office is genuinely local. Track calls per office, not just per firm, so a quiet phone in one city is visible instead of being hidden inside a healthy firm-wide total.

9. Track rankings, calls, and cases per location — never firm-wide

A firm-wide average is the most misleading number in multi-location marketing. Ten consultations a week sounds healthy until you learn that nine came from the home city and one from everywhere else combined.

Track each location separately: its Map Pack and organic rankings for its core searches, its profile views and calls, its website consultations, and — most importantly — its signed cases. A location page that brings in five consultations a month is worth more than a blog post that brings in a thousand visitors and no calls, and a second office that ranks well but signs nothing has an intake problem, not a marketing problem.

Review the numbers per location every month, and let them decide where the next round of work goes. If the second office ranks but does not convert, fix its intake. If the third office converts the few callers it gets but barely ranks, invest in its reviews and local links. A website and a marketing plan are not finished when they launch; they are finished when the numbers say they are working in every city you serve.

Where to start this month

If your firm serves more than one city and only ranks in one of them, do not try to fix everything at once. Start with the changes that matter most.

  • This week: verify that every real office has its own complete Google Business Profile, and that no profile exists for an office that is not real. Check that each profile’s name, address, and phone number match its location page exactly.
  • This month: build or rebuild one genuine location page per city, with real local detail — the courthouse, the attorneys in that office, and the surrounding towns you serve. Ask recent clients at each office for a review on that office’s profile.
  • This quarter: audit citations for every location, add local schema markup to every location page, and start tracking rankings, calls, and signed cases separately for each office.

Clients choose the firm that is visibly present in their city — a real office, real reviews, real local knowledge, and a phone that gets answered by someone who knows where they are. A firm that builds that presence city by city, honestly and completely, will win searches in every city it serves — and keep winning them, month after month.
