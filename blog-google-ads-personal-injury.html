Google Ads for Personal Injury Lawyers: Turn Accident Searches Into Signed Cases

VL
Vertex Law MarketingPublished 17 November 2026 · 10 min read

A person hurt in a car crash does not shop for a lawyer the way most clients shop for anything. They search in pain, on a phone, often from an emergency room waiting area or a kitchen table the same night it happened. They type “car accident lawyer near me” and they call the first firm that looks able to help — usually within minutes, and usually without visiting more than one or two websites.

That urgency is exactly why Google Ads works so well for personal injury firms — and exactly why it can burn money faster than any other kind of law firm advertising. Personal injury keywords are among the most expensive in all of Google Ads, and every click from the wrong searcher is a click your best case never got. The firms that win are not the ones with the biggest budget. They are the ones whose campaigns are built for the way an injured person actually searches, clicks, and calls.

Here is how personal injury firms use Google Ads to turn accident searches into signed cases — and how to stop paying for clicks that were never going to become clients.

1. Accept the price of the click — then make every click count

Personal injury is the most competitive corner of legal advertising for a reason: a single signed case can be worth a great deal, so firms bid accordingly. The result is that the core searches — “car accident lawyer”, “personal injury attorney near me”, “truck accident lawyer” — carry some of the highest costs per click in Google Ads. Our guide to why personal injury keywords cost $100+ per click explains the economics in full; the practical consequence is simple.

At these prices, a campaign that is merely “turned on” is a campaign that is losing money. A broad keyword with a generic ad and a homepage landing page will buy clicks from researchers, students, job seekers, and people in states you do not serve — and each one costs as much as a click from a seriously injured driver in your city. The firms that profit from Google Ads in personal injury treat structure as the budget: tight keyword groups, ads written for one case type at a time, and landing pages that match the search exactly.

That discipline is also what lets a smaller firm compete. You do not need to outbid the biggest firm in your market on every keyword. You need to win the searches that become cases, and stop paying for the ones that do not.

2. Bid on searches that can become cases — in the right order

Not every personal injury search is worth the same money. The highest-intent searches name the accident and the lawyer in the same breath: “car accident lawyer Chicago”, “truck accident attorney near me”, “slip and fall lawyer”. Those are the searches to win first, and to bid most aggressively on, because the searcher has already decided they need a lawyer and is choosing which one to call.

Below those, a second tier of searches is still valuable but should be bid on separately, at lower bids, with different ads: “how much is my car accident case worth”, “average settlement for a rear-end collision”, “do I need a lawyer after a minor accident”. These searchers are earlier in their decision. They may become clients — often after a consultation that answers the question honestly — but they convert at a lower rate, and they should not share a budget with the searches that are ready to call now.

Keep the two tiers in separate campaigns with separate budgets. If they share one budget, the cheaper informational clicks will quietly consume the money that should have bought the urgent ones. And remember that paid search does not work alone: our guide to ranking in the Google Map Pack covers the local visibility system, and the firms that win both channels own the first screen for accident searches.

  • Highest intent: case type + lawyer or attorney + city, and “near me” versions of the same. Bid here first.
  • High intent: specific injuries and situations — “rear-ended by a drunk driver”, “injured in a rideshare accident”, “hurt at work, need a lawyer”. Separate ad groups, matched landing pages.
  • Research intent: settlement values, time limits, fault questions, “do I need a lawyer”. Lower bids, helpful landing pages, and a clear path to a free consultation.
  • Not worth bidding on: free legal advice, legal aid, pro bono, law school or job searches, and do-it-yourself settlement forms. These belong on your negative keyword list, not in your campaigns.

3. Match the ad to the injury, not to the firm

Most personal injury ads read the same: the firm name, “free consultation”, “no fee unless we win”, and a phone number. A searcher who has just been rear-ended skims past that, because it does not tell them whether this firm handles their kind of accident.

Build one ad group per case type — car accidents, truck accidents, motorcycle accidents, slip and fall, pedestrian accidents, wrongful death, medical malpractice — and write the ads for that case type alone. A car accident ad should say car accident, name the free consultation, and say the firm only gets paid if the client recovers. A truck accident ad should say truck accident, because a searcher who was hit by a commercial truck is looking for a firm that understands trucking cases specifically.

  • Use the searcher’s words in the headline. If the search was “car accident lawyer”, the headline should say car accident lawyer, not “injured? We can help”.
  • Say what happens when they call. A free, confidential consultation, no fee unless the firm recovers compensation, and a real person answering — not a form and a wait.
  • Use call assets and location assets. On mobile, most personal injury clicks should become phone calls, not website visits. Make the phone number one tap away, and show the office location so the searcher knows the firm is local.
  • Test call-only ads for the highest-intent searches. When someone searches “car accident lawyer near me” at 9 p.m. from a phone, the fastest path to a signed case is a call, not a landing page. Call-only ads remove the step in between.
  • Keep the tone steady. Fear-based copy and promises about outcomes repel the careful clients you want most — and in many states, advertising rules limit what a firm can promise about results. Say what you will do: listen, review the facts, explain the options honestly, and pursue the best result the law allows.

The same rule applies to the page the ad leads to. An ad about car accidents that lands on a general personal injury homepage is a broken promise, and a searcher in pain will not wait to work out where the car accident information lives.

4. Build landing pages that convert an injured visitor in seconds

A personal injury click is the most expensive click most law firms will ever buy. The landing page has to earn it immediately, because the visitor is hurt, distracted, and comparing you against two other firms in other tabs.

The first screen should answer three things before anything else: do you handle my kind of accident, what does it cost to talk to you, and how fast will someone respond. A phone number that is one tap away, a short form — name, phone, and one line about what happened — and a plain statement that the consultation is free and that the firm only gets paid if it recovers compensation. Everything else — attorney biographies, office photos, past case descriptions — belongs further down, after the visitor has decided the firm might be able to help.

  • One page per case type. The car accident page talks about car accidents: what to do after a crash, how the claim process usually works, what affects timing, and what the first consultation covers. The truck accident page does the same for truck accidents. A page that genuinely answers the searcher’s question is the page that converts — and it is also the page Google rewards with a better quality score, which lowers what you pay per click.
  • Load fast on a phone. A slow page on a mobile connection loses the click you just paid a premium for. Keep images light, remove anything that delays the phone number and the form, and test the page on a real phone, not only on a desktop.
  • Show real people. Real photos of the attorneys and staff, a real office address, and a real phone number. Stock photos of handshakes tell an injured visitor nothing — and in this practice area, anonymity reads as a warning sign.
  • Do not ask for what you do not need yet. Police reports, medical records, and insurance details come after the first conversation, not before it. A form that asks for them up front signals a firm that does not understand the visitor’s situation.
  • Confirm what happens next. After the form is submitted, say exactly what happens: who will call, when, and what they will ask. A confirmation page that says nothing is a missed chance to reassure someone who is already anxious.

5. Cut the wasted spend with negative keywords

In personal injury, the difference between a profitable campaign and a money pit is often the negative keyword list. Because the core terms are so expensive, a single irrelevant click costs real money — and the irrelevant searches are numerous.

Start with the obvious exclusions and review your search terms report every week to find more:

  • Jobs and education: jobs, hiring, salary, law school, paralegal, internship, “how to become a personal injury lawyer”
  • Free and DIY: free legal advice, pro bono, legal aid, “settle my own claim”, settlement calculator, demand letter template, sample settlement agreement
  • Research and news: statistics, famous cases, celebrity accidents, news stories, “what is personal injury law”
  • Wrong places: cities and states you do not serve, and the names of firms you are not — unless you are deliberately and carefully running a competitor campaign, which is a separate decision with its own rules
  • Wrong case types: if you do not handle medical malpractice, exclude it; if you do not take workers’ compensation cases, exclude those terms rather than paying for their clicks

The search terms report is where this work happens. Every week, look at what people actually typed before they clicked your ad, and add every term that could not have become a client to the negative list. Over a month, this single habit can cut a meaningful share of wasted spend — and the savings go straight into the searches that do become cases.

6. Win the call: intake speed is the real conversion rate

Personal injury clients call the firm that answers. A searcher who has just been in a crash will often call two or three firms in a row; the one that picks up first usually gets the case. That makes intake speed — not the ad copy, not the bid — the single biggest lever on your cost per signed case.

  • Answer in minutes, around the clock. Accidents do not keep office hours, and neither do the searches. A real response standard — every call answered live during the day, and a trained answering service or on-call intake after hours — is worth more than a higher bid.
  • Keep the first call short and human. The caller needs to hear that the firm handles their kind of accident, that the consultation is free, and what the next step is. A script that asks for documents before it offers reassurance loses callers.
  • Follow up once, well. A caller who does not answer the first callback is not a lost client. One prompt, polite follow-up — a call and a short message — recovers cases a single attempt would lose. More than that becomes pressure.
  • Track the call, not just the click. Call tracking numbers per campaign, and a note in your intake system of which campaign and keyword produced each call, turn Google Ads from a traffic bill into a case pipeline you can judge.

This is also where many firms discover their ads were working all along and their intake was not. Before raising the budget, check how many calls went unanswered, how long callbacks took, and how many consultations never got booked. Fixing intake is usually cheaper than buying more clicks.

7. Target the geography and the hours where cases happen

Personal injury is a local practice, and your campaigns should be built that way. A firm in one city should not be paying for clicks in another state, and a firm that serves three counties should know which county produces its cases.

  • Target where you actually practise. Set location targeting to the cities and counties you serve, and use “people in your targeted locations” rather than “people searching about your locations”, so a searcher in another state looking up your city does not cost you a click.
  • Bid by location performance. When the data shows one county signs cases at a lower cost than another, shift budget toward it. Over a quarter, that reallocation alone can move your cost per signed case noticeably.
  • Schedule ads for when people search — and when you can answer. Accident searches spike in the evening and after weekend crashes, but if no one can answer the phone at 2 a.m., paying for those clicks wastes them. Either staff the hours or schedule the ads around the hours you can genuinely serve, and use call scheduling so the phone number only shows when someone is there to pick up.
  • Separate mobile from desktop. Most personal injury searches happen on a phone, and mobile clicks convert differently — usually into calls, at a different rate and cost. Keep mobile and desktop performance visible separately so you are not judging a call-heavy mobile campaign by desktop form-fill standards.

8. Budget by signed case, not by click

The question most personal injury firms ask first — how much should we spend on Google Ads — is the wrong question until you know what a signed case is worth to the firm and what you are willing to pay to sign one. Our guide to how much a law firm should spend on Google Ads gives the budgeting framework in full; for personal injury, three rules matter most.

  • Work back from the case, not forward from the click. Decide what a signed case is worth and what you can afford to pay to sign one, then judge the campaign by cost per signed case. A campaign with expensive clicks and a low cost per signed case is a good campaign; a campaign with cheap clicks and no signed cases is a waste of money.
  • Give a new campaign enough budget to learn. A budget so small that it buys a handful of clicks a day will never produce enough data to judge, and pausing it after a week tells you nothing. Fund it properly for a full month, with the structure in place, before deciding whether it works.
  • Reallocate, do not just increase. When one case type signs cases at a lower cost than another, move budget toward it. When one keyword produces calls that never sign, pause it and give the money to the keywords that do. Most personal injury campaigns improve more from reallocation than from a bigger total budget.

Judge the spend monthly, not daily. Click costs move with competition, and a single expensive day means nothing. A month of cost per consultation and cost per signed case, by campaign and by case type, tells you what to expand, what to fix, and what to stop.

9. Measure signed cases, not clicks

Google Ads reports clicks, impressions, and click-through rates by default. None of those is a client. For a personal injury firm, the numbers that matter are consultations booked, consultations that become signed cases, and cost per signed case — by campaign, by case type, and by keyword where the data allows.

  • Connect the ad to the signed case. Use call tracking, form tracking, and your intake or case management system together, so a signed case can be traced back to the campaign that produced it. Without that link, you are guessing which half of the budget works.
  • Import offline conversions. A click becomes a consultation, and a consultation becomes a signed case, days or weeks later. Feeding signed cases back into Google Ads as offline conversions lets the system bid toward the searches that actually produce clients, not just the ones that produce clicks.
  • Review search terms weekly, performance monthly. The search terms report catches wasted spend early; the monthly review of cost per signed case is where the real decisions happen — which case types to grow, which keywords to pause, and where the next dollar goes.
  • Keep the rest of the funnel honest. Ads bring the call, but the website, the intake, and the follow-up sign the case. Where to start this month

You do not need to rebuild everything at once. For most small-to-midsize personal injury firms, this order returns the most signed cases for the least spend:

  • This week: split your highest-intent keywords into their own campaign with matched ads and landing pages, add the obvious negative keywords, and check how many calls went unanswered last week
  • This month: rebuild your top three landing pages — car accidents, truck accidents, and slip and fall — with one-tap calling, a short form, and a clear statement of what happens next; start reviewing the search terms report weekly
  • This quarter: connect signed cases back to campaigns with call tracking and offline conversion imports, then reallocate budget toward the case types with the lowest cost per signed case

Personal injury clients are not looking for the loudest firm in their city. They are looking for the firm that answers, understands their kind of accident, explains what it costs — nothing up front for the consultation, and no fee unless the firm recovers compensation — and follows up when it matters. Be that firm in the ad, on the page, and on the phone, and Google Ads stops being the most expensive line in your marketing budget and starts being the most reliable source of signed cases you have.

Key Takeaways

  • Personal injury keywords are among the most expensive in Google Ads, so campaign structure — not budget size — decides whether the spend produces cases.
  • Split intent into tiers: bid hardest on case type + lawyer + city searches, lower on research questions, and never let the two share one budget.
  • One ad group per case type, with ads and landing pages written for that injury alone; a car accident ad should never land on a general homepage.
  • Negative keywords and a weekly search terms review are the fastest way to cut wasted spend in personal injury campaigns.
  • Intake speed is the real conversion rate: the firm that answers first usually signs the case, so staff the hours your ads run.
  • Target the places you practise, schedule ads around the hours you can answer, and judge mobile and desktop performance separately.
  • Budget by cost per signed case, not cost per click — and feed signed cases back into Google Ads as offline conversions so the system bids toward real clients.
